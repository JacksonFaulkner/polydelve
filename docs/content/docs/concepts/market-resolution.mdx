## Resolution outcomes

Settlement logic lives in `backend/features/settlement.py`. A contract is either a YES bet or a NO bet.

### YES contracts

Three independent win conditions; any one triggers a win, and wins can fire before the deadline:

- **CVE win** — a new CVE with `cvss_score >= cvss_threshold` was published (per `cve_history`) after the contract was created (requires `cvss_threshold` set).
- **EPSS win** — `epss_history` recorded a score `>= epss_threshold` after `created_at` (requires `epss_threshold` set).
- **MAL win** — a non-withdrawn MAL advisory was published for the package after `created_at`.

The deadline passing with none met resolves the contract **expired**, with no payout.

### NO contracts

NO bets are CVSS-only and the inverse of the CVE win:

- **Lost** immediately if a CVE with `cvss_score >= cvss_threshold` is published after `created_at`. No payout.
- **Won** if the deadline passes with no such CVE. Pays `max_payout`.

NO bets are only offered on packages with a CVE in the last 100 days.

### Baskets

A basket contract holds several packages and a `threshold_count`. Each member is checked with the YES rules above; the basket is **won** once `threshold_count` members have won, and **expired** at the deadline otherwise.

## How resolution is triggered

Settlement does not poll OSV live per contract. It reads from tables already populated by upstream ETL jobs:

- `cve_history` — populated by the `cve` job (OSV bulk data)
- `epss_history` — populated by the daily `epss` refresh (FIRST bulk file)
- `mal_advisories` — populated by the `mal` job

For each open contract, it checks these tables for a qualifying row after the contract's `created_at`. Won contracts get `status='won'` and `bits += max_payout`; expired contracts get `status='expired'`; lost NO contracts get `status='lost'`. Expired and lost contracts pay nothing.

## Timing and cadence

Resolution runs as the last step of the ETL refresh (`etl.run refresh`: epss, news, mal, cve, then resolve); `make resolve-contracts` runs it manually. The scheduled Fargate task (EventBridge Scheduler) runs three times a day, at **9am, 12pm, and 5pm ET**. Contracts are not resolved in real time; there is a lag between an underlying advisory/score update landing in the database and resolution being recorded.

EPSS and CVE data refresh independently of resolution (see [Vulnerability Tracking](/docs/concepts/vulnerability-tracking)).

## Edge cases

<Accordions>

<Accordion title="Multiple qualifying conditions">
If a YES contract has both `cvss_threshold` and `epss_threshold` set, either one alone is sufficient to win — they are not both required. The MAL condition is always active on YES contracts.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Disputed or withdrawn advisories">
The system does not automatically re-open or reverse a resolved contract — resolution is final at the time the job runs. Withdrawn MAL advisories are ignored when checked.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Data lag">
Upstream sources (OSV, EPSS bulk file, MAL advisories) can lag their own publish times by hours to days depending on ETL cadence. Resolution is always based on Polydelve's ingested tables, not a live upstream call.
</Accordion>
